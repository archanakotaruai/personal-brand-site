01 — Signature stories

Three problems that shaped how I deliver AI.

Secure government AI

AI-assisted document review in a high-security cloud

“In government AI, the hard part isn't generating an answer. It's security, trust, and getting people to rely on it.”

Challenge
Expert reviewers were spending days on every long, technical document, with thousands arriving at once.
Approach
I led the technical approach for summarization, RAG-based Q&A and automated reviewer matching, with models and data kept inside a government-grade cloud boundary.
What we learned
Hybrid semantic plus keyword search beat pure vector search. Totals are computed in deterministic code; the LLM never does the arithmetic.
Result
A pilot cut review time dramatically, and the pilot users asked to take it to production.
AI that helps people

Lease Buddy: reading a lease before you sign it

It started as my son's idea after he watched families sign leases they couldn't fully read. It's the project closest to my heart.

Challenge
Immigrant tenants were signing binding contracts written in a language and legal register they didn't know.
Approach
I built it end to end: it translates a lease and highlights the clauses a tenant must understand before signing, then answers follow-up questions.
Result
Now used by non-profits serving immigrant communities.

03 — How I work

Five things I believe about shipping AI.

Each one comes from something that broke, or almost did.

01In regulated environments, security is the product.+

A brilliant model nobody is allowed to use delivers nothing. I design the security boundary, the data handling and the approval path first, then pick the model that fits inside them.

02Measure before you trust, including the judge.+

Golden sets, hit rate, MRR, faithfulness. And a human sanity check of the automated grader, because a lenient judge makes a weak system look finished.

03Let code do the math.+

LLMs summarize and reason well; they add numbers badly. Anything that must be exact, like budget totals, goes to deterministic code.

04The scaffolding is the system.+

Guardrails, routing, moderation and approval gates decide whether an agent is production-ready. I spend more time there than on the prompt.

05Teach the team, not just the tool.+

AI adoption sticks when it's built into the sprint: demos, shared learning time and peer teaching. Tools change every quarter; capable teams keep up.
